## 1. Purpose

M7.5 answers exactly one question about a discovered, normalized candidate:

> "Does this appear to represent research that already exists in WiseEvidence?"

It grades the answer as **DEFINITE / PROBABLE / POSSIBLE / NEW**, and — new in
M7.5 — **explains why**, so a human reviewer can adjudicate. It never merges,
deletes, publishes, or classifies. Human review remains the authority.

M7.3 already shipped a conservative graded matcher (`packages/discovery/src/
orchestrator/dedup.ts`) and M7.4A wired it to the database (`DatabaseStudyIndex`).
M7.5 did **not** rebuild that; it **enriched** it with structured explainability,
explicit year-conflict handling, a false-positive guard, and a comprehensive
adversarial/determinism/safety test surface — with **no schema change**.

## 2. Matching hierarchy (unchanged order, now explainable)

| Level | Signal | Verdict | `reasonCode` |
|------:|--------|---------|--------------|
| 1 | Exact canonical DOI | DEFINITE | `DOI_EXACT_MATCH` |
| 2 | Exact persistent id (PMID/PMCID/ARXIV) | DEFINITE | `PERSISTENT_IDENTIFIER_MATCH` |
| 3 | Normalized title + compatible year | PROBABLE | `TITLE_YEAR_MATCH` |
| 4 | Normalized title, year unconfirmed/conflicting | POSSIBLE | `TITLE_EXACT_MATCH` |
| — | No comparable identity to check | NEW | `INSUFFICIENT_METADATA` |
| — | Checked, nothing matched | NEW | `NO_MATCH` |

**LEVEL 5 (fuzzy title similarity) is deliberately NOT implemented** — see §7.

## 3. Decision meanings

- **DEFINITE** — the candidate's *publication identity* (DOI or persistent id) is
  already recorded against a study. It is flagged and routed to review; it is
  **not** merged (see §4, Study ≠ Publication).
- **PROBABLE** — normalized title matches an existing study **and** the candidate's
  year is among that study's known publication years.
- **POSSIBLE** — normalized title matches but the year cannot be confirmed (missing
  on either side, or different). The explanation records the year comparison.
- **NEW** — nothing matched. `INSUFFICIENT_METADATA` distinguishes "we could not
  check" (no DOI, no persistent id, no usable title) from `NO_MATCH` ("checked,
  found nothing").

Every decision carries a structured `explanation`:

```
reasonCode, matchedIdentifierType, matchedIdentifierValue,
titleMatched, candidateYear, matchedStudyYears, yearConflict
```

The reviewer UI (`/admin/imports/[id]`) shows the reason code and the year
comparison ("2022 vs 2023") alongside the existing verdict/matched-by/reason.

## 4. Candidate identity vs research identity

Two **different** duplication questions, never collapsed:

- **A) Candidate identity** — "have we already imported this exact source item?"
  Owned by the candidate store, DB-enforced by the migration-0013 partial unique
  index on `(source_key, source_stable_id)`. M7.5 does **not** touch this.
- **B) Research-level dedup** — "does this candidate represent research already in
  WiseEvidence?" This is the matcher's job (this milestone), decided against
  canonical studies via the read-only `KnownStudyIndex` port.

## 5. Study ≠ Publication

A single study may be reported by many publications (protocol, primary report,
secondary analysis, follow-up, erratum, review, commentary). A DOI/persistent-id
match therefore means *this publication identity is known*, **not** "same study =
merge". The matcher only records `relatedStudyId` and routes to human review; the
existing M7.4B workflow (accept / link-duplicate / reject / correct / defer)
remains the authority. The engine never merges, overwrites, deletes, or publishes.

## 6. Conservative design (false-positive vs false-negative)

**WiseEvidence prefers a missed duplicate over a wrong merge.** A false positive
can destroy provenance; a missed duplicate can be caught in review later. So the
matcher errs toward the *weaker* verdict whenever evidence is incomplete:

- Year unconfirmed ⇒ POSSIBLE, never PROBABLE.
- A near-but-not-exact normalized title ⇒ NEW (no fuzzy matching).
- An empty/punctuation-only normalized title ⇒ never a title match.

## 7. Why LEVEL 5 (fuzzy similarity) is not built

Conservative title similarity was evaluated and **deliberately deferred**:

1. **False-positive risk** — the highest of any level; it directly threatens the
   "prefer a missed duplicate over a wrong merge" principle.
2. **Schema/performance firewall** — a correct similarity lookup needs either a
   `pg_trgm` GIN index (a **new migration** — a STOP condition) or scanning every
   study's title per candidate (an unbounded `SELECT`, explicitly disallowed).

Both are out of M7.5 scope. The `DedupReasonCode` union intentionally omits
`TITLE_SIMILAR`. If similarity is ever authorized it would arrive with its own
migration proposal through the schema firewall.

## 8. Explainability

`reasonCode` is enumerated and auditable; `matchedStudyYears`/`candidateYear`
make the year decision inspectable; `matchedIdentifierType`/`Value` name the exact
identifier. The stored dedup decision (`import_candidate.dedup_decision` JSONB)
carries the explanation, and `service/candidates.ts` parses it defensively (older
rows without an explanation still render). It is an **identity signal only** — it
never implies outcome, quality, confidence, efficacy, or scientific validity.

## 9. Security

- Matching is **server-side**; the `KnownStudyIndex` port exposes only `find*`
  (read) methods — there is no write path. RLS is unchanged and authoritative
  (`DatabaseStudyIndex` reads only; the discovery store writes on the
  `service_role` path, staff-gated).
- No public dedup endpoint, no client-controlled study ids or match decisions, no
  provider secrets, no AI, no arbitrary URL fetching, no RLS bypass.
- Untrusted metadata is canonicalised via `@wise-evidence/domain` and sanitized
  before it reaches the matcher; identifier values are compared as opaque
  parameters (bound SQL — no injection).

## 10. Performance

Indexed, bounded lookups only: DOI/persistent-id via
`idx_identifier_value_canonical`; title via `idx_study_normalized_title`. The
title lookup returns the matched study's years through a correlated aggregate
bounded to that one study (`group by … order by s.id limit 1`) — never
`SELECT * FROM research_study`. No Elasticsearch, Redis, vector DB, or embeddings.
$0 incremental cost.

## 11. Determinism

The matcher is a pure function: same input + same index state ⇒ identical
decision. No randomness, no clock, no network, no AI, no model inference. Verified
by a deep-equality test over repeated runs.

## 12. Limitations (honest)

- No fuzzy/near-title matching (see §7) — genuinely different wordings of the same
  title are reported NEW and rely on human review or an identifier match.
- When several studies share an identical normalized title, the title lookup picks
  one deterministically (`order by s.id`); the match still routes to review.
- Year is compared at 4-digit granularity; a study with no recorded publication
  date yields "year unknown" (POSSIBLE, not PROBABLE).

## 13. Tests

New `packages/discovery/src/orchestrator/matcher.test.ts` (30 tests) plus the
existing `dedup.test.ts` (6). Coverage:

- **Identifiers:** same/different DOI, DOI case & URL forms canonicalise to one
  match, malformed DOI, missing DOI.
- **Persistent identifiers:** same PMID, same PMCID, different id.
- **Title/year:** same title+year → PROBABLE; same title + different year →
  POSSIBLE with explained conflict; missing candidate year; study without a year;
  multi-publication study; punctuation/whitespace/Unicode normalization equivalence.
- **Similarity:** near-duplicate → NEW; empty/punctuation-only title never matches.
- **Study ≠ Publication:** DOI match flags but never merges; a different
  publication of a known study stays reviewable.
- **Insufficient metadata:** no comparable identity → INSUFFICIENT_METADATA;
  URL/SOURCE_ID not treated as persistent id.
- **Adversarial:** HTML/script in title, SQL-like identifier strings, extremely
  long titles, duplicate identifiers.
- **Determinism:** repeated runs deep-equal.
- **Safety:** the port is read-only (`find*` only); classification mutates nothing.

The database-level guarantees (persistence, idempotency, no canonical writes, RLS
matrix) remain covered by the existing PGlite suites, updated for the new decision
shape.
